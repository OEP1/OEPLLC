Consulting services for operations and healthcare teams

Three practice areas, delivered as fixed-price engagements with defined deliverables and a documented handoff.

Practice area 01

Data collection & reporting

Leadership asks a reasonable question — why supply spend jumped, how long intake actually takes, which shift generates the rework — and the honest answer is that nobody knows. Not because the team is careless, but because no one ever designed a way to capture it.

We determine what is genuinely worth tracking, then build collection into the existing workflow rather than bolting it on. If a nurse or a warehouse lead has to open a separate spreadsheet to record something, it will not survive the month.

  • Definition of the small set of measures that drive decisions
  • Collection designed to fit the workflow, not interrupt it
  • Extraction from existing EHR, ERP, or WMS systems where possible
  • One-page management reporting rather than an unused dashboard
  • Data audit method to verify the numbers stay trustworthy

Typical engagement

A focused review of one department or process, producing a written measurement plan, the collection tools themselves, and a reporting format your managers will actually use.

Common triggers

  • Budget variances nobody can explain
  • Conflicting numbers from different departments
  • Reporting that takes days to assemble by hand
  • A new system that nobody trusts yet

Typical duration: 3–6 weeks · Fixed fee

Practice area 02

Supply chain management

Stockouts and overstock are the same failure wearing different clothes. Both come from par levels set by memory, ordering driven by whoever noticed an empty shelf, and no visibility into what is sitting in a back room two floors away.

In clinical settings the stakes are sharper. An expired item is a compliance finding, an untraceable recall is a serious exposure, and some supplies have no substitute. We work through those specifics rather than applying a generic retail inventory model.

  • Par levels rebuilt from actual consumption data
  • Vendor and contract review, including unused GPO terms
  • Receiving and put-away processes that catch errors at the dock
  • Expiry rotation and recall traceability for clinical supply
  • A reorder method your staff can run independently

Typical engagement

A full assessment of inventory, procurement, and distribution for one facility or site, followed by implementation of revised par levels, processes, and controls.

Common triggers

  • Recurring stockouts of critical items
  • Expired product write-offs
  • Supply spend rising faster than volume
  • A survey or audit finding on materials handling

Typical duration: 6–12 weeks · Fixed fee

Practice area 03

Workforce training & onboarding

In most operations and healthcare environments, training is an oral tradition. A new hire shadows whoever is available, absorbs that person's particular habits, and learns the rest by making mistakes. It works, slowly, and it quietly penalizes your best people by adding a second job to their day.

We convert what experienced staff already know into material that teaches without them, then train those staff to run the program so it outlives the engagement.

  • Written procedures for the tasks that actually cause errors
  • A structured ramp with checkpoints at week one, 30 days, and 90 days
  • Competency verification that measures skill rather than attendance
  • Preceptor training so the load is shared and consistent
  • Documentation that stands up to survey and audit requirements

Typical engagement

Design and pilot of a complete onboarding program for one role or department, including all written materials, competency tools, and preceptor training.

Common triggers

  • Turnover concentrated in the first 90 days
  • Errors traced to inconsistent training
  • Senior staff overloaded with training duties
  • Rapid hiring ahead of an expansion

Typical duration: 8–16 weeks · Fixed fee

Frequently asked questions

How is pricing structured?

Fixed fee per engagement, quoted in writing after the initial consultation. You know the total before work begins. Extended implementation support is billed monthly with 30 days' notice to conclude.

Do you work on site?

Yes, and for this work it is usually necessary. Par levels cannot be rebuilt and training cannot be written without observing the work directly. Analysis and documentation happen remotely.

How small is too small?

Not very. A single surgical center, one distribution site, or a single department inside a larger system are all workable. What matters is whether someone has authority to change how the work is done.

How is protected health information handled?

Engagements are structured to avoid PHI wherever possible, since supply and operational data rarely requires it. Where access is unavoidable, we work under your business associate agreement and access policies.

Not sure which practice area fits?

That is what the first conversation is for. Describe the situation and we will tell you honestly whether we can help.
