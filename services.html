Services

Three ways to work together

Most engagements fall into one of these. If yours doesn't, say so — the shape can be adjusted.

Engagements Pricing is fixed per engagement and quoted after the first call.

2–3 weeks · fixed fee

Assessment

A focused review of one area of the business. I interview the people doing the work, look at whatever data exists, and come back with a written picture of what's happening and what I'd change first.

  • Interviews with your team and, where useful, your customers
  • Review of current process, tooling, and reporting
  • A written findings document with prioritized recommendations
  • A working session to walk through it with your leadership

4–8 weeks · fixed fee

Strategy and planning

For when you know roughly what needs to change but not how to sequence it. We build the plan together, so the people who have to execute it actually believe in it.

  • Goal-setting sessions with the leadership team
  • A prioritized roadmap with owners, dates, and dependencies
  • Clear definitions for how you'll know it's working
  • Help communicating the plan to the wider organization

3–6 months · monthly retainer

Implementation support

Plans fail in the execution, not the planning. This is ongoing, hands-on involvement while the change is actually happening — for teams that don't have the internal bandwidth to drive it alone.

  • Weekly working sessions with the team doing the work
  • Unblocking decisions before they stall for a month
  • Coaching for whoever will own this after I leave
  • Monthly progress reporting for your board or stakeholders
Common questions

Before you ask

How is pricing structured?

Fixed fee per engagement, quoted in writing after our first conversation. You'll know the total before anything starts. Retainers are billed monthly with 30 days' notice to end.

Do you work remotely?

Yes, and most engagements run that way. On-site time can be built in where it genuinely helps — kickoffs and working sessions usually benefit from being in the same room.

How small is too small?

Not very. I've worked with teams of six and teams of six hundred. What matters more is whether someone on your side has the authority to make decisions.
